## The Grand Canyon

I think the Grand Canyon is the opposite of Mount Rushmore.

Mount Rushmore is a monument that fits within a frame. 
If you've seen a picture of Mount Rushmore, you basically get the gist.
It's hand-carved slopes transform a mountianside from natural beauty to cultural icon.

The word "Grand" doesn't do the Grand Canyon justice. 
There's a sheer scale to it that makes observation akin to astronomy: 
a reminder of how miniscule a human really is.
No one picture does it justice. 


As someone who recently under-estimated a hike, I appreciated that the signage makes the difficulty of the full hike clear.

<Lightbox collection="trip-2025" image="20250609_143421_IMG_1595.avif" alt="Sign warning not to hike too far at the Grand Canyon" />

I know there's tourist attractions at the Grand Canyon, where they'll bus you into the most picturesque spots, or let you walk on [a glass skywalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canyon_Skywalk).
However, I decided to keep it simple, and just wander at my own pace.
The [Grand Canyon Shuttle](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/shuttle-buses.htm) 
made it easy to explore on a whim.

I loved seeing the mules at work in the Grand Canyon.
They do a few jobs. 
For one, you can book a mule ride for a casual $185.56/person[^mule].
They also carry equipment and supplies to the canyon floor, which is what I believe is happening here.
In this case, I believe they're coming back from the 7.5 mile route[^mile] across the South Kaibab Trail to get to Phantom Ranch,
which is the only lodging below the canyon rim.

[^mule]: Source: https://www.grandcanyonlodges.com/plan/mule-rides/
[^mile]: Source: https://www.theadventurediet.com/blog/a-complete-guide-on-how-to-visit-phantom-ranch-grand-canyons-most-epic-place-to-stay

Also, I want to highlight the incredibly solid trail construction.
This is another example of the outstanding work by the CCC, and continues the theme of durability we talked about earlier: 
it's lasted ~100 years, and it's likely to last at least another 100 years with light maintenance.

Sometimes, the scale of the Grand Canyon blends together. 
It's easy to lose sight of how incredibly massive it is, compared to humans.
Take a look at the picture below, and see if you can spot the two grainy specs of humanity.
You can use the button below to reveal their location.

<ShowPeople client:load />

Eleven Native-American tribes, including the Basketmaker-Pueblo, Hopi, Hualapai, and Yavapai-Apache,
have a history of living in or near the Grand Canyon. 
[The history is fascinating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Grand_Canyon_area#Early_history), 
though I don't feel qualified to explain it here.
Nonetheless, as I see how small humans are compared to the canyon trails,
I also reflect on how impressive it is that humans lived
in the canyon thousands of years before there were tourist-friendly trails.

## Zion

I enjoy hiking and exploring nature as a way to get away from 
the hustle and bustle of urban life. 
However, most city streets are less densely packed than Zion national park.
Let's take a look at the numbers.


|             | Miles of Trails                    | Annual Visitors (2024) <sup>[source][stats]</sup>| Visitors/Mile |
|:------------|-----------------------------------:|:------------------------------------------------:|--------------:|
| Zion        | 90 <sup>[source][zion]</sup>       | 4,946,592                                        |    **54,962** |
| Yosemite    | 750 <sup>[source][yose]</sup>      | 4,121,807                                        |     **5,496** |
| Yellowstone | 1,100 <sup>[source][yell]</sup>    | 4,744,353                                        |     **4,313** |

[zion]: https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/backpacking.htm
[yell]: https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/trails.htm
[yose]: https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/trails.htm
[stats]: https://irma.nps.gov/Stats/

Obviously, it's naïve to assume that visitors are equally spread out over all trails, but the point is in the proportions.
Zion recieves an *order of magnitude* more visitors per mile than other parks with similar attendance.
