### Meet the car

Let me introduce you to the car that will be taking me on this grand adventure.

... good photo of car ...

She's a 2007 Hyundai Sonata. I call her "Sunny".

For years, her only purpose in life has been to get me to work and back again &mdash; about a 20 minute drive. 
However, for the next month, she will be driving thousands of miles on long interstates, treacherous mountain passes, and terrifying dirt and gravel roads.
I've come to really appreciate what she's capable of, to the extent that now just putting around town now feels like a waste of potential.

However, I didn't start the trip with any confidence. 
Two weeks before I left, my car broke down while I was running errands.
I could feel my car losing power, so I pulled into a Walmart parking lot, where it then wouldn't start.

<Lightbox collection="trip-2025" image="car-on-flatbed.jpg" alt="My car on a AAA flatbed, two weeks before the start of my trip." /> 

I had AAA tow it and give it an inspection. They couldn't find anything wrong.

I later got two more independent inspections which agreed my car would be ready for the trip.

While the initial problems certainly made me nervous, I had already booked all my lodgings,
and the two independent inspections convinced me to just hope nothing bad happens.

I believe this is what we call *"foreshadowing"*.

## Packing

Let's take a look at what we're working with to start the trip:

<Lightbox collection="trip-2025" files={[{image: "trunk.jpg", alt:"The trunk of my car"}, {image: "trunk-open.jpg", alt:"The trunk of my car, filled with the supplies I'd need for the trip."} ]} /> 

I planned to camp for most of the time I was travelling, which both helped to make the experience more affordable and was a ton of fun. 
To that end, I brought a tent, a cook stove, cooking supplies, and everything I'd need to live out of my car.
Notably, what's not pictured here is my suitcase full of clothes, which got shoved on top of everything else.
In the cabin of my car, I brought a 10 gallon jug to fill with water, which was the only way I stayed hydrated during the breaks between potable water.
